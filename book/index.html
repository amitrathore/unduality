Unduality

When the Mind Ends

By Amit Rathore, in conversation with The Psychologist
Identity to be announced as the book develops.

Twenty-two dialogues about psychology, consciousness, and what becomes possible when thought reaches the limits of what it can know.

In development · Follow the inquiry

What ends?

The mind does not stop functioning.

Thought, memory, imagination, judgment, personality, and practical responsibility remain. The book examines something more specific: the assumption that a separate internal manager stands behind experience, authoring thought and controlling life.

If that manager cannot be found, does the person diminish—or become more fully available?

The two voices

Two authors. Neither accepts the other’s premises. Both are willing to look.

Psychology + direct inquiry

The
Psychologist

The Psychologist is the working name for the book’s second author, a psychologist whose identity will be announced as the work develops.

The role is not to validate Unduality’s central proposition, but to challenge it: expose unsupported leaps, resist oversimplification, identify safety limits, and ask whether each claim survives contact with psychological knowledge and direct experience.

What survives when both perspectives are questioned?

First principles + direct inquiry

Amit
Rathore

Amit Rathore is an engineer, author, creator, and entrepreneur. Through iRealization, he has pursued a first-principles investigation of reality, perception, identity, and consciousness—beginning with what can be directly known rather than inherited belief.

In Unduality, he brings a contemporary inquiry into mind, identity, consciousness, and reality into open examination.

The premise

Psychology asks how the self becomes well. Unduality asks whether the self being repaired is what we think it is.

The aim is not to defeat psychology or prove nondualism. It is to discover which claims survive psychological knowledge, direct observation, and sustained disagreement.

The conversations are the inquiry. We challenge, refine, and shape them into the book while remaining faithful to what the inquiry reveals.

We are not simply writing a book together. We are discovering the book together.

Complete working outline

Four movements.
Twenty-two dialogues.

The outline is a map, not a promised conclusion. Each chapter begins with a question and keeps both psychological knowledge and direct observation open to revision.

Part I · 01—06

The Psychological Human

  1. 01

    Why Are You Here?

    What is therapy attempting to accomplish? What do we actually mean by psychological health?

  2. 02

    What Is a Psychological Problem?

    Before solving psychological problems, what makes something a problem?

  3. 03

    Thought

    What is a thought? Where does it originate? Who creates it?

  4. 04

    The Past

    Where does the past exist now? An inquiry into memory, conditioning, history, and narrative.

  5. 05

    The Future

    How can someone suffer today because of something that has not happened?

  6. 06

    Emotion

    What remains when emotion is investigated as sensation, interpretation, memory, thought, and resistance?

Part II · 07—12

The Construction of Me

  1. 07

    Childhood

    How does a human organism acquire the psychological experience of being “me”?

  2. 08

    Identity

    Is identity what I am, or information about myself appearing within experience?

  3. 09

    The Wounded Self

    Trauma, attachment, shame, rejection, and psychological injury. What is wounded, what remains, and when can nondual inquiry become bypassing?

  4. 10

    The Inner Voice

    Why do thoughts appearing in the first person seem to be me?

  5. 11

    Control

    Can we locate the manager apparently sitting behind experience and controlling the mind?

  6. 12

    Free Will

    If we examine an actual decision closely enough, where does it originate?

Part III · 13—18

The Machinery of Suffering

  1. 13

    Fear

    What distinguishes immediate biological danger from psychological fear?

  2. 14

    Desire

    What are we really seeking through achievement, possession, recognition, and relationships?

  3. 15

    Resistance

    What happens when experience meets the thought: “This should not be happening”?

  4. 16

    Relationships

    Are two people encountering one another, or are two accumulated psychological representations interacting?

  5. 17

    Happiness

    Why does happiness acquired from circumstances continually disappear?

  6. 18

    The Search for a Better Me

    Do self-improvement, therapy, achievement, optimization, and spirituality share the assumption that I must become other than I am?

Part IV · 19—22

Beyond the Psychological Self

  1. 19

    The Observer

    Can we actually find an observer separate from what is being observed?

  2. 20

    Consciousness

    What can be known outside consciousness? What does that question reveal about reality, and where does inference begin?

  3. 21

    Who Are You?

    Body, brain, personality, history, thinker, observer. What remains when every description of “me” is observed as another event in experience?

  4. 22

    Unduality

    What changes when thought no longer occupies the center of experience? Does the person diminish—or become more fully available?
